Reposition Drugs. De-Risk Trials.
Powered by Multi-Omics AI.

We develop multi-omics models to help research partners prioritize candidate therapies, biomarkers, and experiments. Our initial focus is autism spectrum disorder.

What We Do

We integrate measurements across the gut microbiome and host biology to investigate disease heterogeneity and biological change over time.

Our goal is to help partners prioritize therapeutic hypotheses and design the experiments and biomarker studies needed to test them.

  • Drug repositioning: Prioritize existing compounds for experimental evaluation against candidate biological mechanisms
  • Experimental design: Evaluate candidate stratification strategies and measurements to inform study design
  • Integrated molecular view: Multiple biological systems modeled together over time, not a single snapshot in isolation

Modeling Biology Over Time

We are developing longitudinal models that connect molecular measurements with neurodevelopmental outcomes and quantify uncertainty in biological trajectories.

Our research follows multiple biological systems over time. We aim to improve prediction and identify candidate mechanisms and measurements for experimental validation.

Prediction and intervention effects are different questions. We assess which causal conclusions the available data and assumptions can support, and which require further experiments.

Why This Approach Is Different

Our approach connects multi-omics measurements, longitudinal prediction, and experimental prioritization. We evaluate the contribution of each data layer rather than assume that more measurements always improve prediction.

  • Multiple biological layers, not just one: Joint analysis of microbial and host measurements can reveal complementary biological signals
  • Changes over time, not just a snapshot: We develop models of biological trajectories and evaluate their predictive performance
  • Testable causal hypotheses: We assess causal assumptions and prioritize experiments to distinguish candidate biological explanations
  • Uncertainty-aware modeling: We develop tools to connect biological measurements with outcomes and make modeling assumptions explicit
  • Backed by published science: Our scientific foundations include research on ASD-associated profiles, microbe-metabolite relationships, and compositional data analysis in Nature Neuroscience, Nature Methods, and Nature Communications

Research Participant in Wellcome LEAP FORM

We participate in Wellcome LEAP's FORM program (Foundations of a Resilient Microbiome)—a $50 million global initiative investigating the link between the gut microbiome and neurodevelopment in early life.

Our research investigates relationships between early-life microbiome function and neurodevelopmental outcomes, including autism. Establishing which relationships are causal is a research objective.
